About Me

I am a Ph.D. student in Digital Innovation and e-Health (started in October 2026) at the University of Bari Aldo Moro, conducting research within the Computational Intelligence Laboratory (CILAB) at the Department of Computer Science.

My research is centered around Artificial Intelligence for Digital Mental Health (DMH). Traditional psychiatric and clinical assessments still rely predominantly on episodic visits and standardized psychometric questionnaires, providing only an incomplete, episodic snapshot of an individual's mental state. Digital Mental Health opens the transformative opportunity for continuous, ecologically valid monitoring via wearable sensors and digital technologies capable of acquiring physiological, behavioral, acoustic, and contextual biomarkers.

While AI offers tremendous promise for analyzing these heterogeneous, high-dimensional streams, its real-world clinical adoption faces major obstacles. My doctoral research is dedicated to addressing these through three core methodological pillars:

1. Learning under Limited Labeled Data

Overcoming clinical annotation scarcity through benchmarked comparative analyses of self-supervised learning (SSL), semi-supervised learning, transfer learning, and modern foundation models on physiological time-series (e.g. HRV, EDA).

2. Multimodal Learning & Sensor Fusion

Investigating the distinct predictive contributions of multiple data modalities (physiological, acoustic, behavioral, contextual) and formulating advanced multimodal fusion architectures capable of handling asynchronous and heterogeneous data flows.

3. Explainable & Trustworthy AI (XAI)

Bridging the black-box gap by comparing post-hoc explainability techniques (LIME, SHAP, counterfactuals) with intrinsically interpretable models (such as fuzzy linguistic representations), validated through human-centered studies involving clinical specialists.

The overarching goal is to formulate a unified methodological framework for designing reliable, robust, and transparent AI systems for continuous mental health monitoring, advancing their translation into clinical practice.

Publications

AIPHEA @ IEEE WCCI 2026
Explaining Unsupervised Acoustic Patterns in Parkinson’s Patients’ Speech through Fuzzy Linguistic Representations
Gabriella Casalino, Giovanna Castellano, Maria Creanza*, Katarzyna Kaczmarek-Majer, Gianluca Zaza
1st Workshop on AI in Predictive HEAltH: architectures for prevention (AIPHEA 2026), co-located with 2026 IEEE World Congress on Computational Intelligence (IEEE WCCI 2026), Maastricht, The Netherlands, June 21-26, 2026. CEUR Workshop Proceedings (to appear).
Abstract: Speech-based analysis is a powerful tool for investigating Parkinson's disease, yet unsupervised representations often lack interpretability in medical settings. This paper presents an interpretable unsupervised framework combining Non-Negative Matrix Factorization (NMF) with fuzzy linguistic modeling. By translating latent acoustic factors and sample contributions into perceptual linguistic concepts, the framework provides transparent, multi-level explanations (latent factors, clusters, and individual speech recordings) without enforcing rigid class boundaries.
